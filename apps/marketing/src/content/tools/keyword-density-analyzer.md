## The number nobody should be optimising

Keyword density is the most durable bad metric in SEO. It made sense when ranking was closer to term
counting, and it has survived for two decades because it is easy to compute and produces a
satisfying-looking percentage.

The percentage means very little. What the underlying data shows is genuinely useful, which is why
this tool reports counts and phrases rather than a verdict against some ideal figure.

## The two failures it does catch

**A page that never says what it is about.** This is far more common than stuffing, especially in
copy written to sound impressive. The subject is implied by the surrounding design, the nav, the
brand — everything except the text. Run the check and the top phrases are generic verbs. A reader
figures it out from context; a model extracting a passage has nothing specific to lift.

**A page that says it too often.** Usually inherited content, or work from a supplier paid by the
word against a density brief. It is obvious once you see the same phrase at 4% of a 1,200-word page,
and it reads exactly as badly as it measures.

## How to read the output

Look at the two- and three-word phrases first, and ask one question: if you only saw this list, would
you know what the page is for? That is close to the question a search engine and an assistant are both
answering, and it is a better test than any percentage.

If a phrase is missing, the fix is one mention in the title, one in the opening sentence, one in a
heading. If a phrase is overwhelming, the fix is to delete instances rather than add synonyms around
them.
