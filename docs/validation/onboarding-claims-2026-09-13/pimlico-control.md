# Signs of mains water leaks and how to check for them

A mains water leak is a break or crack in the pipe that carries water from the street main into your home, and mains water leak detection starts with a simple meter test before any specialist equipment gets involved. Left unnoticed, this kind of leak can push up your water bill, wash out paving, and soak floors or walls without a single dripping tap in sight. The checks below use only what's already at your property: your stopcock, your water meter, and your own eyes and ears.

## What counts as a mains water leak?

The "mains" supply is the pipe running from the water company's main in the street, through your meter (if you have one), up to the internal stopcock inside your property. A leak anywhere along that run, whether under the pavement, your front garden, or beneath your floor before the stopcock, counts as a mains water leak rather than a leak on your internal plumbing. The Leak Detection Company notes that this covers both the incoming external supply and the internal cold supply pipe up to the point where it splits off to taps and appliances, which is [why detection often has to work through floors or hard standing](https://www.theleakdetectioncompany.co.uk/mains-water-leaks/) rather than exposed pipework.

## What are the signs of a mains water leak?

Most mains leaks show up as a pattern of small clues before they become obvious. Look out for:

- A water bill that's noticeably higher than usual with no change in how you use water

- Low or dropping water pressure across every tap in the house, not just one

- Damp or unusually lush, boggy patches in the garden, driveway, or paving during dry weather

- Damp patches, mould, or blown plaster on internal walls or floors that have appeared or worsened recently

- A hissing, knocking, or bubbling sound near where the supply enters the property, especially if it stops when the stopcock is turned off

- A formal Section 75 notice from your water supplier under the Water Industry Act 1991, which gives you a set window, usually seven to fourteen days, [to repair a suspected leak](https://www.theleakdetectioncompany.co.uk/mains-water-leaks/)

## How to check if your water main is leaking

You don't need any equipment to run a first check. Work through these steps in order.

- **Turn off every tap and water-using appliance in the property.** Expected result: no water should be moving through the system while you take readings.

- **Find your internal stopcock, usually under the kitchen sink, and turn it off.** Expected result: this isolates your indoor plumbing so you're testing the supply pipe on its own.

- **Locate your water meter and record the reading, including all dials.** Expected result: you have a fixed baseline number.

- **Wait 30 minutes to an hour without using any water, then read the meter again.** Thames Water's guidance uses the same 30-minute window [to isolate leaks between the meter and the stop valve](https://www.thameswater.co.uk/help/water-and-waste-help/leaks/leaks-at-home/identifying-leaks). Expected result: a second, comparable reading.

- **Compare the two readings.** Expected result: if they match, there's unlikely to be a leak between the meter and your stopcock. If the second reading is higher, or the meter's flow indicator is still spinning, water is passing through with nothing turned on, which points to a leak on the supply pipe.

- **Follow the supplier’s meter and inside stop-valve test.** If the readings differ, the cited Thames Water instructions indicate a leak somewhere between the meter and the inside stop valve. Ask the supplier to clarify responsibility for any repair.

A positive result on this test, combined with any of the visible or audible signs above, is a reasonable basis to arrange a professional inspection rather than keep testing repeatedly.

## How does a plumber find a hidden mains leak?

Once a leak is suspected but not visible, engineers move to detection methods designed to pinpoint the exact location without opening up the whole pipe run. [Specialist non-invasive tools](https://www.theleakdetectioncompany.co.uk/mains-water-leaks/) commonly used for this include acoustic microphones that listen for the sound of escaping water through soil or concrete, tracer gas pumped through the empty pipe to find where it escapes at the surface, thermal imaging to spot temperature differences caused by water saturation, dye tracing, and moisture mapping across floors and walls. These techniques narrow a leak down to a specific point before anyone digs or lifts flooring, which limits the disruption compared with excavating along the whole pipe route.

### Comparing your options

| Method | What it tells you | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meter and stopcock test | Whether a leak exists between the meter and your stopcock | Confirms a leak but not its exact location |
| Visual and audible checks | Damp patches, pooling, or hissing that suggest where a leak has been running | Some leaks run silently underground with no surface sign |
| Acoustic, tracer gas or thermal detection | The precise point along the pipe where the leak sits | Requires a specialist engineer and dedicated equipment |

## Is a water leak detector worth it, and what does professional detection cost?

A plug-in or battery water leak detector placed near a washing machine, boiler, or under a sink is useful for catching an internal appliance leak early, but it won't detect a leak on the buried supply pipe outside those rooms. For a suspected mains leak, the meter test above is the free first step; if it confirms a leak, professional detection is what narrows down the exact spot. There's no single UK-wide figure for what that costs, since it depends on how deep and how long the pipe run is, whether it sits under hard standing, and how far the leak has to be traced, so it's worth asking a local plumber or leak detection specialist for a quote based on your property rather than relying on a generic number.
