How to compare email marketing platforms for small business: features, pricing, deliverability, and ease of use

Email marketing platforms for small business are tools that let you design, send, and automate campaigns without a developer or an internal IT team. Comparing them means checking four things: which features you'll actually use, what a plan costs at your contact volume, whether the platform protects your sender reputation, and how fast you can launch a first campaign. The right answer depends on your list size, your team's technical comfort, and how many channels beyond email you plan to use.

Email marketing platforms for small business: what features matter most?

Start by listing the tasks you need the platform to do, not the features it advertises. Most small business senders need five things: a drag-and-drop editor with ready-made templates, marketing automation for welcome series and follow-ups, contact segmentation, sign-up forms to grow the list, and basic performance reporting. According to emailvendorselection.com's 2026 review of over 30 email marketing platforms, a user-friendly drag-and-drop editor and visual automation builder are the two features that separate platforms people actually keep using from ones they abandon.

As an example of how these categories look in practice, Brevo's Starter and Standard plans bundle a drag-and-drop editor with industry-specific templates, unlimited multi-step automation workflows, advanced segmentation by behavior and custom events, an AI content generator for subject lines, and AI send-time optimization that sends each contact's email when they're most likely to engage, according to Brevo's pricing page. Treat this as one illustration of what "automation" or "segmentation" can include when you're reading a features list, not a benchmark every platform must match.

What should you check for email deliverability?

Deliverability is the share of emails that reach the inbox instead of spam or bouncing. When comparing platforms, Mailtrap's 2026 startup email tools comparison recommends checking for built-in email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), bounce and spam-complaint monitoring, and real-time delivery notifications, since these determine whether a sender's reputation stays healthy as volume grows. Ask any vendor you're evaluating how they handle hard bounces and whether dedicated IPs are available for high-volume senders.

As one data point, Brevo states its email marketing runs on dedicated deliverability infrastructure with a 99% email delivery rate, monitored sender reputations on shared IPs, and automatic exclusion of hard bounces, according to Brevo's marketing platform page. That figure is Brevo's own reported metric for its infrastructure; ask any platform you're comparing for its equivalent number and how it's measured before treating deliverability as a solved problem.

How easy is a platform to set up and use?

Ease of use is hard to judge from a features list, so test it directly. Open a free trial or free plan, build one real campaign end to end (a welcome email or a promotion), and time how long it takes from login to a scheduled send. Count how many clicks it takes to add a segment, insert a merge field, and preview on mobile. A platform that requires a support ticket to do any of those steps will slow down every campaign after the first one.

Customer feedback can be a useful signal here, with caveats: a business owner quoted on Brevo's marketing platform page, Paul Lacitinola of Vintage Camper Trailers, said the campaign builder is "very straightforward" and let him "create professional emails in minutes." That's one user's testimonial published by the vendor, not an independent usability study, so weigh it alongside your own trial rather than in place of it.

A worked example: comparing platforms for a 2,000-contact newsletter

Here's a hypothetical scenario to show how the criteria above interact. Imagine a boutique retailer with 2,000 email contacts who wants to send a weekly newsletter plus an automated welcome series for new subscribers, with no in-house developer.

  • Feature check: the retailer needs a drag-and-drop editor, one automation workflow (welcome series), and basic segmentation by purchase history, ruling out any tool without visual automation.

  • Price check: at 2,000 contacts, the retailer compares each shortlisted vendor's plan that covers that contact count and the email volume a weekly newsletter to that list implies (roughly 8,000 emails a month), then checks whether crossing into a higher tier is imminent as the list grows.

  • Deliverability check: the retailer asks each vendor whether SPF, DKIM, and DMARC setup is guided or self-serve, since a solo owner without technical staff needs the platform to walk them through authentication.

  • Ease-of-use check: the retailer opens a free trial for two or three shortlisted platforms and builds the welcome series in each, comparing how many steps it takes and whether templates fit a retail brand.

The platform that wins isn't necessarily the cheapest one; it's the one that lets this retailer launch the welcome series without a support call and stays affordable as the list grows past 2,000 contacts.

There's no single universal ranking, because "best" depends on which of the criteria above matters most to your business. Emailvendorselection.com's 2026 review tags Moosend as "best for small businesses," Brevo as "best for free email marketing," MailerLite as "best for starting with email marketing," and ActiveCampaign as "best for marketing automation," reflecting that reviewer's testing rather than a fixed hierarchy. Use tags like these as a shortlist starting point, then run your own trial against the features, pricing, and deliverability checks above before you decide.

Choosing your platform

Comparing email marketing platforms for small business comes down to matching four checks, features, pricing at your actual volume, deliverability infrastructure, and hands-on ease of use, against your own contact list and team. No single vendor wins every check for every business, so shortlist two or three platforms, run a free trial for each, and build one real campaign before you commit.
