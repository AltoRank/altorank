How to choose a conversational AI platform: pricing, training, and channel coverage compared

A conversational AI platform is software that lets a support team build, train, and deploy AI agents that answer customer questions over chat, email, voice, or messaging apps, instead of routing every message to a human first. Choosing one comes down to three practical questions: how the pricing scales with your volume, how much work it takes to train the AI on your business, and which channels it actually covers. This guide compares those three factors across a few named platforms so you can run your own evaluation.

What is the best conversational AI platform?

There is no single best option, because the platforms built for phone-heavy operations look nothing like the ones built for chat and email. Crisp combines a shared inbox, AI agents, and a knowledge base under one flat monthly price, aimed at teams centralizing chat, email, WhatsApp, and social messaging. Retell AI is built around voice calls, billed by the minute, aimed at teams running phone support or outbound campaigns. Fini layers on top of an existing helpdesk and charges per resolved ticket, aimed at teams in regulated industries that need an audit trail. The right one depends on which channel carries most of your support volume today.

Pricing models: flat rate, pay-as-you-go, or per resolution

These three platforms charge in fundamentally different ways, and that difference matters more than any single number. A flat rate protects your budget as volume grows. A per-minute or per-message rate rewards low usage but scales linearly with it. A per-resolution rate ties cost directly to outcomes but assumes you already have a helpdesk for the AI to plug into.

What is the best free conversational AI?

Crisp offers a "Free Forever" plan with 2 seats and a website chat widget, though it does not include AI credits at that tier. Retell AI is not free but starts every account with $10 in usage credits and 20 free concurrent calls, so you can test real calls before paying. Fini does not offer a free tier; instead it backs its paid plan with a 90-day, resolution-based guarantee. ElevenLabs lists a free plan among its named tiers on its agents pricing page, but the specific usage allowance is not detailed in what's publicly visible, so check that page directly if voice is your primary channel.

How AI agents get trained on your business

Training method affects how fast you can go live and how much technical help you need. Three approaches show up across these platforms:

  • Crisp trains its AI by crawling your website automatically, and you can also import PDFs and write knowledge base articles. From there, you build the agent's logic in a no-code workflow builder before deploying it to a channel.
  • Retell AI syncs a knowledge base to keep the agent updated automatically, and its visual builder includes simulation testing so you can run edge cases before a real caller does.
  • Fini connects to your existing helpdesk (Zendesk, Intercom, Salesforce, HubSpot, and others) through one-click OAuth, trains on your tickets and knowledge base, and, per its own site, aims to go live in 14 days with full autonomy targeted by day 30.

None of these skip the setup step entirely. Every one of them still needs a knowledge base, a workflow, or a connected helpdesk before the AI agent can answer anything reliably.

A worked example: picking a platform for a small e-commerce support team

Say a Shopify store has six support agents and needs to cover a website chat widget, email, and WhatsApp, at roughly 1,000 customer conversations a month. Here's how the comparison plays out.

First, check which plan actually unlocks the required channels. Crisp's WhatsApp coverage sits inside the omnichannel inbox, which starts at the Essentials plan ($95/month), not the cheaper Mini plan. Second, check seats: Essentials includes 10 seats, which covers the six-person team with room to grow before hitting the $10-per-additional-agent add-on. Third, check the AI credit allotment against expected automated volume: Essentials includes around 450 automated conversations for $25 in AI credits. If the team expects to automate close to that number, it fits; if forecasted automation volume is meaningfully higher, confirm current overage terms directly with Crisp before committing.

Now compare that against the alternatives. If this store's real bottleneck were a phone support line instead of chat and WhatsApp, Retell AI's per-minute billing would apply instead, and the team would model cost using Retell's own calculator rather than a flat monthly fee. If the store already ran a helpdesk like Zendesk and needed audit-grade compliance for regulated transactions, Fini's per-resolution pricing would be worth testing. For this scenario, a text-and-messaging-first store with a small team, the flat per-workspace model is easier to budget against because the price doesn't move with conversation count within the plan's allotment.

How to test a platform before you commit

  1. Sign up for a trial that matches your real channel mix. Crisp offers 14 days of full access with no card required, so you can connect your actual website and inbox rather than a sandbox.
  2. Connect your knowledge base the same way you would in production: crawl your site, upload your actual PDFs, or connect your live helpdesk.
  3. Deploy the agent to one real channel first, whichever carries the most volume, and watch how it handles messy, off-script questions rather than scripted demo queries.
  4. Track how many conversations the AI resolves against your plan's included allotment (for Crisp) or your accumulated usage cost (for Retell AI or Fini), so you know before renewal whether you'll need a higher tier or hit overage charges.

Whichever platform you shortlist, run this test with your own data before signing an annual contract. A demo conversation rarely predicts how an AI agent handles a real customer who is frustrated, vague, or asking three things at once.
