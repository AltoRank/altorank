How to care for merino wool shoes: washing, drying, and storage tips

How to care for merino wool shoes comes down to three habits: wash them gently, air dry them fully, and store them somewhere cool and dry. Merino wool shoes can usually be cleaned by hand or on a delicate machine cycle, but heat from a tumble dryer can shrink the wool fibers, so drying method matters as much as washing method. The steps below walk through a full care routine, plus what to skip.

Can merino wool shoes be washed?

Yes. According to care instructions published by Merinos, a merino wool shoe brand, its merino wool shoes are fully washable by hand or machine. That guidance applies to that brand's own construction, so it's worth checking the care tag inside your specific shoes, whether they're from Allbirds or another maker, before assuming the same method applies. Materials like leather trim, glue, or foam midsoles can change what's safe.

How to wash merino wool shoes

Whichever method you use, start the same way: wipe off loose dirt with a dry towel, then pull out the insoles and laces so they can be cleaned separately. Insoles and laces typically hold onto more sweat and grime than the upper, so washing them apart gets better results.

Machine washing steps

  1. Wipe the shoes down to remove loose dirt and debris.
  2. Remove the insoles and laces; set them aside to hand wash in the sink.
  3. Place the shoes in a mesh or linen laundry bag to protect them and your machine.
  4. Run the delicate or wool cycle with cold water, per the Merinos care guidance cited above.
  5. Skip the dryer entirely and move straight to air drying (details below).

Hand washing steps

  1. Wet the shoes with warm water.
  2. Use a soft cloth and a mild soap solution to spot-clean dirty areas.
  3. Rinse gently and avoid soaking the shoes for extended periods.
  4. Air dry only.

For scuffed soles specifically, the Merinos care page notes that a melamine foam cleaning pad (the kind sold as a "magic eraser") works well on rubber outsoles. Avoid bleach or any detergent with brightening agents, since these can discolor or weaken wool fibers.

How to dry merino wool shoes without damaging them

Air dry merino wool shoes only. A tumble dryer's heat can shrink the wool, permanently changing the fit of the shoe. Instead, reshape the shoes gently after washing, keep them out of direct sun or off a hot radiator, and let them dry at room temperature before wearing them again or putting them away.

How often should you wash merino wool shoes?

There's no fixed washing schedule to point to here. Instead, wash based on visible dirt, scuffing, or odor rather than a calendar date. One relevant note from the Merinos care instructions: wearing merino wool shoes in wet conditions may cause odor, which is a useful signal to clean them sooner rather than wait.

How to store merino wool shoes between wears

Make sure shoes are completely dry before putting them away; storing damp wool invites musty odor and can affect shape. A cool, dry closet or shoe rack out of direct sunlight is a safer spot than a damp mudroom or a hot car trunk, since prolonged heat exposure is already the reason tumble drying is discouraged. Loosely stuffing the toe area with acid-free paper or a shoe tree can help the shoes hold their form between wears, especially if they were recently washed.

Merino wool shoes hold up well when the wash, dry, and storage steps are kept simple: gentle cleaning, air drying, and a dry resting spot. Whatever brand you wear, including Allbirds, checking the care tag or the brand's own instructions first is the safest starting point, since construction details can change what's safe for the shoe.
