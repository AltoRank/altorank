How to care for merino wool shoes: washing, drying, and storage tips

Knowing how to care for merino wool shoes starts with one fact: most merino wool sneakers are fully washable, either by hand or by machine, according to Merinos' shoe care guide. The short version: wipe off loose dirt, wash on a cold delicate cycle or by hand, then air dry only. Never tumble dry, since heat can shrink the wool.

Can merino wool shoes be washed?

Yes. Merinos describes merino wool shoes as fully washable, whether that means a full machine cycle or a hand wash by the sink. The tradeoff is time and effort: a machine wash plus air drying takes longer to plan around than a quick hand wipe, so it makes sense to save the machine for shoes with dirt across the whole upper and stick to hand washing for regular touch-ups.

How to machine wash merino wool shoes

Machine washing merino wool shoes works when the steps are followed in order, per Merinos' care instructions:

  1. Wipe the shoes down with a towel to remove loose dirt before they go near water.
  2. Remove the insoles and laces. Wash these separately by hand in the sink.
  3. Place the shoes in the washing machine on the delicate or wool cycle, using cold water. A linen bag around the shoes protects them during the spin cycle.
  4. Air dry only. Do not tumble dry, since heat can shrink merino wool.
  5. Reinsert the insoles and laces once everything is completely dry.

How to hand wash merino wool shoes

For lighter cleaning or spot treatment, hand washing is the gentler option. Merinos recommends washing in warm water with a soft cloth, and treating any dirty spots with a mild soap solution. As with machine washing, air dry only, never in a tumble dryer.

For scuffed soles specifically, Merinos notes that a Magic Eraser-style cleaning pad works well to lift dirt and scuff marks without scrubbing at the wool upper.

What is the best detergent to use on merino wool?

Skip bleach and any detergent that contains brightening agents. Merinos' care instructions specifically warn against both, since they can affect the wool fibers or the shoe's color. A mild soap solution, used sparingly on visible dirt, is the safer choice for hand cleaning.

Avoid soaking merino wool shoes in water for extended periods, too. Brief contact during a wash cycle is fine; sitting in a soapy basin for an hour is not.

How to dry merino wool shoes without damaging them

Air drying is the only drying method Merinos recommends, for both machine-washed and hand-washed shoes. Tumble drying introduces heat and tumbling motion that can shrink merino wool, which changes the fit permanently.

In practice, that means setting the shoes somewhere open and ventilated, away from a direct heat source like a radiator or vent, and letting them dry fully before wearing them again. Wearing merino wool shoes while still damp is worth avoiding on its own: Merinos notes that wearing them in wet conditions can lead to odor.

How to store merino wool shoes between wears

Merinos' published care guide focuses on washing and drying rather than long-term storage, so there is no manufacturer-specific storage routine to point to here. The safest general practice is making sure shoes are completely dry before putting them away, since the same source ties dampness during wear to odor, and stored moisture likely carries a similar risk.

Beyond that, a cool, dry, ventilated spot out of direct sunlight avoids the same heat exposure the care guide warns against during drying. If storing a pair of Allbirds merino wool shoes for an extended stretch, keeping them loosely laced and out of a sealed plastic bag gives any residual moisture somewhere to go.

Cleaning mistakes to avoid

  • Bleach or brightening detergents: can affect merino fibers and color, per Merinos' care instructions.
  • Tumble drying: heat and tumbling can shrink the wool.
  • Extended soaking: sitting in water longer than a wash cycle is discouraged.
  • Wearing shoes wet: linked to odor buildup.

Caring for merino wool shoes comes down to gentle washing, air drying only, and dry storage in between wears. Skip the bleach, skip the tumble dryer, and let shoes dry fully before putting them back on or putting them away. Following these steps helps merino wool shoes, including Allbirds' merino wool styles, hold their shape and comfort over repeated wears.
