Multi-Platform Social Media Management Tools for Agencies: Comparing Collaboration Features

Multi-platform social media management means publishing, scheduling, and reviewing content for every client channel from one dashboard instead of logging into five native apps. For an agency, the real test isn't whether a tool can post to Instagram and LinkedIn, it's whether a designer, a strategist, and a client can review and sign off on a post without a chain of emails. This article compares how Buffer, Planable, and Statusbrew structure that collaboration layer, and what to check before adding Hootsuite or Sprout Social to your shortlist.

Buffer: Per-Channel Pricing With Team-Wide Approval Workflows

Buffer connects to Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky, YouTube Shorts, Pinterest, Google Business, Mastodon and X from a single workspace. That breadth suits agencies juggling clients on different platforms without needing a separate login for each network.

The collaboration detail that matters most for agencies: Buffer's Team plan is priced at $10 per month per channel, billed yearly at $120 per channel per year, and it includes unlimited team members, approval workflows, and custom access and permissions. Because pricing is per connected channel rather than a flat per-seat fee, one agency user described it directly: "Buffer's per-channel pricing allows me to be flexible and save money if a client leaves. They're never locked in indefinitely," according to Alexandrea Bowman of Sapphire Social, quoted on Buffer's pricing page. The Free plan, by comparison, includes 10 scheduled posts per channel that refill anytime, with advanced analytics unlocked on the Essentials and Team tiers.

Planable: Client Workspaces Built Around Approval Chains

Planable organizes everything around a workspace per client or brand, with its own pages, calendar, team, campaigns, and approval flow contained inside. That structure suits agencies that need to keep client work fully separated rather than pooling every channel into one shared queue.

According to Planable's pricing page, the Basic plan ($33 per workspace per month) offers two approval types: None (publish without a review step) and Optional (approvals available but not required). The Pro plan ($49 per workspace per month) adds a third, Required, where at least one approver must sign off before anything publishes. Enterprise goes further with multi-level approvals that stack steps in order, for example team lead, then client, then legal.

Statusbrew: Assignment and SLA Workflows for Growing Agencies

Statusbrew's Premium plan bundles "Assignment & approval workflows" alongside "Team & SLA reporting," according to Statusbrew's pricing page. That combination suits agencies that need to route incoming comments and messages to specific team members and track response-time service levels, not just approve outgoing posts before they go live.

Statusbrew also lists a separate agency plan starting "From $49/mo/client," which the page describes as including unlimited spaces, shareable calendars, and reports. The exact dollar figure for the standard Premium tier wasn't shown as a number in the page text reviewed here, so confirm the current rate directly on Statusbrew's site before budgeting a client roster against it.

Hootsuite and Sprout Social: What Still Needs Verification

A 2026 G2 roundup listed Hootsuite starting at $99 per month and Sprout Social starting at $199 per month, describing Hootsuite for "strong post-scheduling and analytics" and Sprout Social for "a unified social inbox." Those are a third-party snapshot from one review, not Hootsuite's or Sprout Social's own current pricing pages, and the excerpt doesn't detail how either tool's approval routing or permission tiers actually work.

Before shortlisting either for a multi-client approval workflow, open each vendor's own pricing page and ask two questions directly: does a plan enforce a required sign-off step before a post can publish, or is approval optional and easy to skip under deadline pressure? And how many client brands or workspaces does a given plan actually allow, since that number changes the real per-client cost.

If clients need strict separation and a hard "nothing publishes without sign-off" rule, Planable's Required approval tier or Statusbrew's assignment workflow map more directly onto that requirement than a flat team price does.

What Is the Best Platform for Managing Multiple Social Media Accounts?

There isn't one best platform for every agency, because the right answer depends on how your pricing model matches your client roster: per channel, per workspace, or per client. Before deciding, check three things on the vendor's own current pricing page: the full list of supported networks, whether approval is a required step or an optional one, and what a plan actually costs once you multiply it across your real channel or client count, not just its advertised starting price.

Making the Call

For an agency weighing collaboration features specifically, the choice comes down to how your client relationships are structured. Buffer's per-channel Team plan fits agencies that add and drop client channels regularly and want approval workflows bundled into one flat rate per channel with unlimited team members. Planable's per-workspace model fits agencies that want a hard Required approval gate isolated by client. Statusbrew's assignment and SLA features fit agencies whose bottleneck is routing incoming messages to the right person, not just approving outgoing posts. Whichever direction fits, verify the current terms on each vendor's own pricing page before you commit a client roster to it.
