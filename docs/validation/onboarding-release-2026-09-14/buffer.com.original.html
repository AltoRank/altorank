Multi-Platform Social Media Management Tools for Agencies: Comparing Collaboration Features

Multi-platform social media management means scheduling, publishing, and engaging across several social networks, like Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, and X, from one dashboard instead of logging into each app separately. For an agency running client accounts, that's only half the job. The real test is whether a teammate can draft a post, a client can review it, and someone with the right permission can approve it before it goes live, without an email thread. Here's how three named tools handle that collaboration layer, and how to test one before you commit.

What Should Agencies Look for in a Collaboration Workflow?

Before comparing tools by name, it helps to know what to check on any pricing page. Four things decide whether a tool actually works for a multi-client agency team:

  • Platform coverage: does it publish to every network your clients use, from Facebook and Instagram to newer ones like Threads and Bluesky?
  • Approval workflows: can a draft be routed to a client or manager for sign-off before it posts, or does every user have publish access by default?
  • Custom access and permissions: can you limit a team member or client contact to only the channels they're supposed to see?
  • Team member and pricing structure: is the plan priced per user, per channel, or a flat team seat, and how many people does that actually let you add?

Buffer: Per-Channel Pricing With Built-In Approval Workflows

Buffer is a social media management platform for creators, small businesses, agencies, and multi-brand teams to publish, schedule, and engage across channels from a single dashboard. According to Buffer's site, it connects to Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky, YouTube, Pinterest, Google Business Profile, Mastodon, and X.

The collaboration features agencies need sit on Buffer's Team plan. Per Buffer's pricing page, the Team plan costs $10 per month per channel ($120 billed yearly) and includes unlimited team members, access levels, and content approval workflows, plus custom access and permissions. The Free plan (3 channels, 10 scheduled posts per channel, refillable anytime) and the $5-per-month Essentials plan both cap out at one user account and don't include approval workflows or custom permissions, so an agency that needs a client to review drafts before publishing needs the Team tier specifically, not the cheaper plans.

Buffer also includes a shared community inbox for replying to comments across channels from one dashboard, and support described on its site as coming from real people rather than bots. Zapier's 2026 roundup of social media management tools flags per-channel pricing as Buffer's standout feature, which suits agencies that add and drop client channels as contracts start and end.

Hootsuite: A Fully-Featured Option for Larger Client Rosters

Hootsuite shows up on both major roundups as the more fully-featured, higher-priced option. Zapier's 2026 list names it its best fully-featured social media management tool, while G2's Spring 2026 Grid Report snapshot, cited in its social media management roundup, describes it as best for strong post-scheduling and analytics, with a drag-and-drop content calendar and AI caption suggestions, starting around $99 per month as reported there.

Hootsuite suits agencies that want deeper scheduling and analytics tooling layered on top of basic publishing. Neither source reviewed here breaks down Hootsuite's specific approval-workflow or permission structure, so confirm seat counts, approval steps, and permission levels on Hootsuite's own current pricing page before weighing the cost against Buffer's Team tier.

Sprout Social: Built Around a Unified Team Inbox

Sprout Social is positioned differently in the same G2 roundup: best for a unified social inbox, with a centralized Smart Inbox and more advanced reporting, starting around $199 per month as reported by G2. That price point and inbox focus point toward agencies managing a high volume of client conversations across channels, rather than agencies primarily focused on lightweight scheduling.

As with Hootsuite, the sources reviewed here describe Sprout Social's category strengths and starting price, not its specific approval-workflow or permission mechanics. Confirm those directly on Sprout Social's own pricing page against the same checklist above.

How to Test a Tool's Collaboration Features Before You Buy

Picture a five-person agency running content for eight clients across Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, and Facebook, where each client's marketing manager needs to approve a post before it publishes but shouldn't see any other client's channels. Run this same test on any tool you're evaluating:

  1. Connect only the channels one client actually needs.
  2. Invite a second teammate at a lower permission level than the admin.
  3. Draft a post and route it through the approval workflow.
  4. Check whether that teammate's view is actually limited to the assigned client's channels.
  5. Note how many total team members the plan allows before you'd need to upgrade.

On Buffer's Team plan, this maps directly to connecting the client's paid channels, inviting their contact as a teammate, using custom access and permissions to restrict their view, and routing drafts through the built-in approval workflow. For Hootsuite or Sprout Social, the same five steps will tell you whether their permission model matches this scenario, since that detail isn't broken out in the roundups cited above.

For agencies whose main friction point is client approval before publishing, Buffer's Team plan is the one of these three with approval workflows and custom permissions documented on its own pricing page, priced per channel rather than per seat. If your bigger pain point is a high-volume shared inbox or deeper analytics reporting, weigh Hootsuite's or Sprout Social's category strengths against their higher reported starting prices, and run the five-step test above before you sign a contract.
